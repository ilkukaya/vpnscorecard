Setting up a VPN is much easier than it sounds. For most devices it takes less than five minutes.

## Step 1: Choose a VPN

Pick a provider with an audited no-logs policy, apps for all your devices and a money-back guarantee so you can test it. Our [rankings](/) and [VPN finder quiz](/quiz/) can help. Avoid unknown free VPNs from app stores — see [are free VPNs safe?](/guides/are-free-vpns-safe/)

## Step 2: Create your account

Sign up on the provider's official website. Long plans are usually much cheaper per month, but a monthly plan is fine if you want to try first. Note the refund window.

## Step 3: Install the app

Always download apps from the provider's website or the official app store.

### Windows and Mac

1. Download the installer from the provider's website.
2. Run it and follow the prompts. On macOS, allow the VPN configuration when asked.
3. Sign in with your account.

### iPhone and iPad

1. Install the provider's app from the App Store.
2. Sign in and tap **Connect**.
3. Tap **Allow** when iOS asks to add a VPN configuration.

### Android

1. Install the app from Google Play.
2. Sign in and tap **Connect**, then accept Android's VPN connection request.
3. Optional: in Android settings, enable **Always-on VPN** and **Block connections without VPN**.

### Smart TVs and streaming sticks

Many providers offer apps for Android TV and Amazon Fire TV. For TVs without VPN apps, use a VPN on your router instead.

### Routers

A router VPN protects every device in your home, including consoles. It requires a compatible router and some patience; follow your provider's router guide step by step.

## Step 4: Connect

Tap **Connect** or **Quick connect**. The app picks a fast server near you. To check it works, visit our [What is my IP](/tools/what-is-my-ip/) tool — you should see the VPN server's location instead of your own.

## Step 5: Change these settings

- **Kill switch: on.** Blocks internet traffic if the VPN drops, so your real IP is never exposed. [Learn more](/guides/what-is-a-kill-switch/).
- **Protocol: WireGuard (or the provider's version of it).** Usually the fastest and most modern option. [Compare protocols](/guides/wireguard-vs-openvpn/).
- **Auto-connect on untrusted Wi-Fi: on.** So you are protected in cafés and hotels without thinking about it.
- **Threat/ad blocker: optional.** Many providers block known malicious domains.

## Troubleshooting

- **Slow speeds?** Pick a server closer to you or switch protocol. See [does a VPN slow down your internet?](/guides/does-a-vpn-slow-down-internet/)
- **A site blocks you?** Try another server or use split tunneling to exclude that app.
- **Can't connect on a network?** Some networks block VPN ports; switch protocol or contact support.

## Use it lawfully

VPNs are legal in most countries, but not all. Check [VPN legality by country](/vpn-legality/) before using a VPN abroad.
