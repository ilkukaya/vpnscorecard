"No logs" is the most important promise a VPN makes — and the easiest to fake. Here's how to tell whether it's real.

## What counts as a log?

- **Activity logs:** websites visited, DNS queries, traffic content. A trustworthy VPN never keeps these.
- **Connection logs:** timestamps, your real IP address, the server you used, data volume. The best VPNs keep none or only minimal, anonymous aggregates.
- **Account data:** your email and payment details. Needed to run a subscription. Some providers, such as Mullvad, even let you pay without an email address.

## How to verify the claim

### 1. Independent audits

A recognised firm inspects the provider's servers, configuration and policies and publishes a report. Repeated audits are better than a single one. Our [methodology](/methodology/) only gives full privacy credit for audited policies.

### 2. RAM-only servers

Servers that run entirely from memory lose all data when rebooted, which makes long-term logging much harder.

### 3. Real-world tests

Court cases and seizures where a provider could not produce user data are strong evidence.

### 4. Transparency and incident handling

Look for transparency reports, warrant canaries and honest disclosure of past incidents.

## Warning signs

- A privacy policy that contradicts the marketing.
- "No logs" with no audit, ever.
- Free services with no clear business model.
