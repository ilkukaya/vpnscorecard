Yes, a VPN usually makes your connection a bit slower — but with a good provider, most people won't notice during everyday browsing and streaming.

## Why a VPN costs speed

- **Encryption** takes processing power on your device.
- **Distance:** your traffic travels to the VPN server before reaching the website.
- **Server load:** busy servers share bandwidth among many users.
- **Protocol overhead:** older protocols like OpenVPN are heavier than WireGuard.

## Seven ways to speed up your VPN

1. **Switch to WireGuard** or your provider's WireGuard-based protocol. See [WireGuard vs OpenVPN](/guides/wireguard-vs-openvpn/).
2. **Connect to a nearby server.** Use "quick connect" or pick your own country.
3. **Try a different server** in the same city if one is slow.
4. **Use a wired connection** or move closer to your Wi-Fi router.
5. **Use split tunneling** for apps that don't need the VPN.
6. **Update the app and your device.**
7. **Restart your router** if speeds are poor even without the VPN.

## Measuring properly

Test your speed without the VPN first, then with it connected to a nearby server. Repeat a few times; results vary with time of day. Our [What is my IP](/tools/what-is-my-ip/) tool confirms the VPN is working.
