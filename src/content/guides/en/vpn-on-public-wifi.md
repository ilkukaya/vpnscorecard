Airport lounges, hotels and cafés all offer free Wi-Fi. It is convenient — but you don't control who runs it or who else is connected.

## The main risks

- **Fake hotspots ("evil twins").** An attacker creates a network with a believable name like "Airport_Free_WiFi". Everything you send passes through their device.
- **Traffic monitoring.** The operator of a network can see which domains you visit and any unencrypted data.
- **Redirects and phishing pages.** A malicious network can send you to fake login pages.
- **Other devices on the network.** Poorly configured networks let devices see each other.

## How HTTPS helps — and where it stops

Most websites now use HTTPS (the padlock), which encrypts page content. But HTTPS doesn't hide which domains you visit, doesn't protect apps that skip encryption, and can't stop you entering your password on a convincing fake site.

## How a VPN helps

A VPN encrypts **all** traffic from your device — browser, apps, DNS lookups — and sends it to the VPN server. Someone on the same Wi-Fi sees only scrambled data. Turn on your VPN app's **auto-connect on untrusted networks** feature so it happens automatically. See [how to set up a VPN](/guides/how-to-set-up-a-vpn/).

## A simple public Wi-Fi checklist

1. Confirm the exact network name with staff.
2. Prefer networks with a password (WPA2/WPA3).
3. Turn on your VPN before doing anything else.
4. Keep your operating system and browser updated.
5. Turn off automatic joining of open networks and file sharing.
6. Use multi-factor authentication on important accounts.
7. When in doubt, use your phone's mobile data instead.
