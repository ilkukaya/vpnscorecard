A **VPN (virtual private network)** is an app that creates an encrypted tunnel between your device and a server run by the VPN company. Everything you do online travels through that tunnel before it reaches the wider internet.

## How a VPN works, step by step

1. You open the VPN app and connect to a server — for example, one in Frankfurt.
2. The app encrypts all traffic leaving your device.
3. The encrypted traffic travels to the VPN server. Anyone in between — the café Wi-Fi, your mobile carrier, your internet provider — sees only scrambled data going to one server.
4. The VPN server decrypts the traffic and sends it to the website you requested.
5. The website sees the VPN server's IP address, not yours.

Replies come back the same way, in reverse.

## What a VPN protects you from

- **Snooping on public Wi-Fi.** Hotel, airport and café networks can be monitored by whoever runs them — or by someone pretending to be them. A VPN encrypts your traffic so they can't read it.
- **Your internet provider's view of your browsing.** Without a VPN your provider can see which domains you visit. With a VPN it only sees that you are connected to a VPN.
- **IP-based tracking and profiling.** Websites and ad networks use your IP address to estimate your location and link visits together. A VPN swaps your IP for a shared one.
- **Some network restrictions.** On school or office networks that block certain services, a VPN can restore normal access — as long as you are allowed to use one there.

## What a VPN does not do

A VPN is a useful privacy tool, but it is often oversold:

- **It doesn't make you anonymous.** If you log into your accounts, those services still know who you are.
- **It doesn't stop malware or phishing.** Some VPNs include blockers for known malicious sites, but you still need updates, good passwords and common sense.
- **It moves trust, it doesn't remove it.** The VPN company can technically see your traffic metadata instead of your internet provider. That is why independent no-logs audits matter so much — see [what a no-logs VPN is](/guides/no-logs-vpn-explained/).
- **It doesn't make illegal activity legal.** Laws still apply.

## What makes a VPN trustworthy?

When we score VPNs, the biggest factors are:

- **An independently audited no-logs policy** — proof, not just a promise.
- **Jurisdiction** — where the company is based and which laws apply to it.
- **Modern protocols** such as WireGuard, which are fast and well-studied.
- **Security features** such as a kill switch and DNS leak protection.
- **Clear pricing** and a real money-back guarantee.

## Free vs paid VPNs

Running servers costs money, so a free VPN has to pay for itself somehow — often with ads or data collection. A few reputable companies offer limited free plans funded by paying customers. Read [are free VPNs safe?](/guides/are-free-vpns-safe/) before installing one.

## The bottom line

A VPN is a simple way to encrypt your connection on untrusted networks and keep your browsing private from your internet provider. Choose one with an audited no-logs policy, use it on public Wi-Fi, and remember that it is one layer of protection, not a magic invisibility cloak.
